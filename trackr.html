TRACKR | Coaching Platform for Fitness Coaches

A shared place for coaches and clients to plan workouts, record sessions and follow progress, replacing the spreadsheet, form and chat stack most coaches run today.

ROLECo-Founder · Product
TIMELINEAug 2026 – Present
STATUSPreparing beta launch
Problem

Online coaches are capped by admin, not demand: every new client adds hours of formatting plans, chasing check-ins and digging through chats.

What I did

Interviewed 8 coaches, wrote the PRD (positioning, pricing, metrics, launch gates) and built the coach web app and client mobile app with AI tools.

Outcome

01The Problem

A typical online coach runs their business across four disconnected tools. Nothing connects them, so the coach becomes the integration layer, and that work grows with every client.

JobTool todayWhere it fails
Deliver the planSpreadsheetUnreadable on a phone mid-set; no logging
Collect check-insOnline formResponses sit in a sheet nobody opens
CommunicateChat appCoaching decisions buried in chat history
Track progressManual notesNo trend view; rebuilt from memory

On the other side, the client can’t reliably answer two questions: what am I doing today, and am I making progress?

02User Research

I interviewed 8 fitness coaches about how they plan, deliver and review training. The clearest finding: scattered tools cap how many clients a coach can take on. That reframed the product from “an app for coaches” to a measurable goal: raise the number of clients a coach can handle without lowering quality.

No Unified ViewCoaches react to whoever messages loudest, not whoever needs help most.
Silent ChurnA disengaged client stays invisible until they quit.
Manual Plan UpdatesOne programming change across a roster costs hours.
No Trend ViewProgression decisions are made on recall, not data.
Admin Crowds Out CoachingQuality slips once a roster outgrows what one person can track.

03Positioning

Incumbent coaching platforms compete on breadth (payments, video, white-labelling) and mostly price in USD. TRACKR competes on depth in the loop coach and client repeat every day, and on India-first pricing.

01Client-side speedOpen, see today’s session, log, done. Target: today’s workout on screen in under 2 seconds.
02One coaching workspacePlan training, manage clients and review progress in one place; reuse programs across clients.
03Built for teams from day oneEvery workspace is an org, from one independent coach to a coaching business with several.

04Scope: What We Said No To

A small team ships by choosing. These were explicitly left out of v1, each with a reason, so the core loop could be excellent.

Out of scope for v1Why
Sales CRM and discovery callsA different buyer moment; TRACKR starts after coach and client agree to work together.
Video callingMeet and Zoom work fine; no way to differentiate.
Open-ended chat inboxRebuilding WhatsApp with fewer features; feedback is tied to sessions instead.
Payments and accountingLarge compliance surface for unclear v1 value.
White-labellingThe incumbents’ battleground; expensive and not our edge.

05The Solution

Write the plan once, and every client gets their own copy.

PlanProgram builderExercises, sets, reps and rest; reusable templates and a start date per client.
TrainClient appToday’s session first, exercise demos and set-by-set logging.
ReviewFollow-up listHistory and a ranked list that puts the clients who need attention first.

What it replaces

Copying last week’s spreadsheet for every client→Assign a program once; each client gets their own copy.
Asking “did you train today?” in a chat→Clients log every set, and the coach sees it the same day.
Scrolling threads to find who’s slipping→A follow-up list puts the clients who need you first.

06Pricing & Business Model

Coaches already price per client, so TRACKR does too, and it gets cheaper per client as the roster grows. That rewards exactly the behaviour we want: coaches taking on more clients.

North-star metric: weekly logged sessions per active client. It captures both sides: the coach published something worth doing, and the client did it.

09Building with AI

I own the product and build it with AI coding tools: writing requirements, reviewing flows in the running product, and directing Claude Code and OpenAI Codex across the coach web app and the client mobile app. That compressed idea-to-working-product into under 3 months.

10Key Takeaways

  • Interviews should change the plan. Coach conversations moved the problem from “coaches need an app” to “scattered tools cap client capacity”, which has a measurable outcome.
  • Depth beats breadth early on. Saying no to payments, video and chat kept the product small enough to ship and sharp enough to explain.
  • Write the metric and the gate before the feature. Adherence and retention targets make it easy to decide what not to build next.
  • When building is fast, product judgement is the bottleneck. AI tools shift the hard part to knowing exactly what to ask for and noticing when the result is wrong.

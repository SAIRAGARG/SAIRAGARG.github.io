YouTube Video-to-Article AI

A tool that turns any YouTube video into a skimmable article, with screenshots placed next to the words that explain them. Built for Andrej Karpathy’s LLM Challenge.

First pages of three generated articles. Each was written and illustrated by the pipeline from the video alone. Click one to open the full article.

ROLEBuilt end to end
CONTEXTAndrej Karpathy’s LLM Challenge
STACKPython · OpenAI · Streamlit
Problem

Long videos are hard to skim, search or quote. A transcript alone loses the slides, code and scenes that carry half the meaning.

What I did

Designed and built a pipeline that transcribes the video, picks the frames that matter, describes them with a vision model and writes an illustrated article.

Outcome

2-hour videos become finished, screenshot-rich articles in under 5 minutes, tested on sports, TV, chess and documentary footage.

01The Problem

In early 2024 Andrej Karpathy challenged builders to turn his 2-hour lecture into a book chapter automatically: readable text, with the right screenshots in the right places. Video is linear. You can’t skim it, search it or pick up where an idea starts, and the transcript misses whatever was shown on screen.

The bar was an article a person would actually choose to read instead of watching, produced fast enough to be worth it.

02How It Works

Four stages, from raw video to a finished article.

StageWhat happensModel or method
ListenThe video is split into 5-minute chunks, each transcribed at the same time, then stitched back with corrected timestamps.OpenAI Whisper
LookA frame is sampled every 5 seconds and near-duplicates are dropped, so only frames that show something new survive.SSIM image similarity
UnderstandEach kept frame is described using the transcript of the same section, so the description matches what is being said. Code on screen is written out.GPT-4 Vision
WriteTranscript and frame notes become one article with a title, table of contents, headings and screenshots in place.GPT-4 Turbo

03Key Trade-offs

Fewer frames, lower costEvery frame sent to the vision model costs time and money. Dropping frames that look almost the same as the last one (similarity above 0.9) removes most of a talking-head video before any model call.
Chunk for speedTranscribing one long file is slow. Splitting into 5-minute chunks and processing them in parallel is what gets a 2-hour video under 5 minutes.
Context in, accuracy outA screenshot on its own is ambiguous. Giving the vision model the transcript from the same moment keeps each caption tied to what the speaker is explaining.
Simple beats cleverI also tried ranking frames by sharpness and contrast. It failed on shorter videos, so I shipped the similarity filter alone.

04What I’d Do Next

  • Cut the cost per video further by sending only the most informative frame from each section to the vision model.
  • Run more model calls in parallel to bring long videos down further.
  • Test open-source models in place of GPT and compare article quality against cost.

05Key Takeaways

  • In an AI product, the cheapest model call is the one you don’t make. Filtering the input mattered more than tuning the prompt.
  • Multimodal output is only as good as its alignment. Text and image have to describe the same moment, or the article falls apart.
  • Test outside the brief. Running it on sports, TV and documentaries, not just lectures, showed it worked for any video.
